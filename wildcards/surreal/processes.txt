# Process estrangement (Man Ray, Lee Miller): an ordinary subject, one photographic process. Nothing impossible is shown.
solarisation: tones partly reversed, a thin dark line running along every bright edge, skin turned silvery
a cameraless photogram: the subject laid on the paper, recorded as white silhouettes and grey translucent shadows on black
a double exposure on one frame: the same subject twice, offset by a step, both exposures equally solid
a triple exposure of one movement, each position a little fainter than the last
a mirror held along the centre line, so the subject is completed by its own reflection and the join is visible
a negative print: light and dark exchanged, everything else exactly as photographed
infrared black-and-white film: foliage white, sky almost black, skin pale and waxy
a long exposure: everything still is sharp, everything that moved is a smear or has vanished
photographed through old rippled window glass, so straight lines waver
a paper print that was folded, carried in a pocket and flattened again, creases crossing the subject
reticulated emulsion: the gelatin cracked into a fine net over an otherwise ordinary picture
a contact sheet: the same subject in twelve small frames, one frame marked in red grease pencil
a shadow-only photograph: the subject is outside the frame and only its shadow crosses the scene
an extreme close-up that makes an ordinary surface read as landscape
bleach-bypass: silver retained, colours drained to near monochrome with hard blacks
a flash photograph at night: the subject flat and overlit, the background dropping to black
photographed from directly overhead, plan view, with shadows as long as the things are tall
